What the LLVM/MLIR Community Gave Me, and What I Want to Give Back

Today this PR was merged, and I'm now one of the co-maintainers of the MLIR Vector dialect.

When I started studying compilers, LLVM looked like a project and a community full of brilliant folks. I remember looking at it and thinking: wow, one day I'd love to contribute and be part of this. Since I started contributing to the Vector dialect, I've learned a lot, mostly thanks to the folks who took the time to review my work, explain things to me, and show me the right direction.

It's been an intense and beautiful time, and the community welcomed me more than I expected. And it still is. I have a lot to learn...

Something about my involvement. One of my RFCs is about unifying masking in the Vector dialect. It got very useful feedback, and I learned a lot from the discussion. We track this work, and most of what's going on in Vector right now, in this index of meta-issues. If you want to know where the dialect is going, it's a good place to start.

In October, thanks to the LLVM Foundation, I went to my first LLVM Developers' Meeting in Santa Clara, and to the MLIR Meetup. Finally meeting in person the folks I only knew from code reviews and Discourse was one of the best parts of the year.

But, why do I do this? Honestly, I'm not doing it to get a job or anything like that. I do it because I really like it, and because I want to help a community that gave me so much, in my small way. Over the past few months, I've spent more time reviewing other folks' patches than writing my own, and I've really enjoyed it.

Of course I still write code too. I'm working on the implementation of the masking RFC myself, and you can follow it in this meta-issue. We're also working on some research projects based on MLIR that I hope to share soon, so I'm also contributing to other parts of MLIR related to them.

Being a co-maintainer is a real honor for me, but it doesn't change much in what I do. I'll keep reviewing patches, answering questions, and keeping track of open issues so that nothing gets lost, with the same consistency and care. Yes, I'm literally obsessed with tracking things, for various reasons :'D

Thank you. To Andrzej (GitHub): thank you for your mentorship, your patience, and for believing in me. I learned so much from you about Vector, Linalg, and much more, but most of all how to be a good member of an open source community. To all the LLVM and MLIR folks who reviewed my patches, shared ideas on an RFC, or let me review theirs: thank you. You make this a great place to learn.

If you're thinking about joining. If you're a student or just getting started, and LLVM looks too big or too scary, please don't let this stop you. I felt the same not long ago. What I found is a community that really cares about helping folks grow. Folks take the time to explain things, give feedback, and guide newcomers, even when they're busy. Nobody expects you to know everything on day one.

Pick a small issue, send a PR, or ask a question on Discourse or Discord. There are also fuzzer-found issues, which are... (OK, I'm joking.) And you know, you can also review PRs yourself. Nobody will stop you ;) If you're interested in Vector, feel free to ping us on a PR or something. You can also find other ways to contact me on my website. I'm also happy to jump on a call, as I've already done with some folks. I'll be happy to help, the same way others helped me.

Thank you, really.
